## Summary
18-year-old creator Jenny Hoyos shares her data-driven blueprint for engineering viral YouTube Shorts, focusing on visual hooks, tight script readability, and structured storytelling.

## Key Takeaways
- Aim for a 5th-grade reading level or below in video scripts to maximize audience comprehension, substituting jargon like 'profit' with simple descriptions.
- Design the first frame of a Short visually so it acts as its own title and thumbnail, making the concept instantly understandable even with the sound off.
- Structure the intro of every Short with a strong hook followed by a foreshadowing line that sets explicit viewer expectations for the outcome.
- Use 'But / Therefore' narrative progression to build cause-and-effect tension instead of passive 'And then' storytelling.
- Trim non-essential seconds at the end of a video; eliminating even 1 second of drop-off can significantly raise overall retention and trigger algorithmic reach.
- Adapt content strategy per platform: YouTube Shorts prefer story-driven content, TikTok favors ultra-dense fast-paced information, and Instagram Reels require high visual shareability.

## Actionable Frameworks
### Shorts Opening Structure
A 3-part intro technique consisting of a visual-first Hook (0-1s), a Foreshadowing line that sets viewer expectations (1-3s), and a Transition line that maintains pacing without breaking momentum.

### But / Therefore Storytelling
A narrative rule where story beats are linked by conflict ('but') and resolution/action ('therefore') rather than sequential listing ('and then'), driving higher viewer retention.

### Idea Filter Funnel
A structured selection process that filters 100 raw ideas down to 50 based on personal interest and feasibility, 25 based on virality mechanisms, and finally 10 after editor review.
