## Summary
Learn six high-level CapCut editing techniques—such as focus shifting, glass texture screen recording, film framing, and parallax depth—to transform simple videos into cinematic, Vox-style documentary edits.

## Key Takeaways
- Create dynamic focus-shift effects by separating elements into compound layers, keyframing position and blur transitions, and applying speed-easing curves.
- Make boring on-screen research compelling by overlaying scanned documents using the 'Multiply' blend mode, speeding up playback, and adding audio risers.
- Elevate text design by selecting professional documentary fonts like Special Elite, Tafel Sans Pro, or Helvetica Black Condensed, and setting text animation duration between 1.5 to 2 seconds.
- Age modern footage into authentic historical archives using the 'Film Frame 2' filter combined with lowered saturation, elevated contrast, and added particle/fade effects.
- Give raw screen recordings a realistic monitor-lens look by adding an RGB glass texture overlay (such as TextureLabs Glass 127) using 'Color Burn' blend mode at roughly 39% opacity.
- Build a 3D parallax feel by copying location/scale attributes between background and foreground compound clips while slightly offsetting scale and position keyframes.

## Actionable Frameworks
### Screen Recording Texture Overlay Method
Place an RGB pixel/glass texture image over a screen recording in CapCut, set the blend mode to 'Color Burn' (or similar), and lower opacity to around 30-40% to mimic footage shot off a physical monitor.

### Rack Focus Layering Workflow
Isolate foreground and background subjects using background removal and rectangle masks with ~20px feathering, compound the clips, and keyframe opposite blur values (0 to 50 and 50 to 0) along with eazed motion curves.
